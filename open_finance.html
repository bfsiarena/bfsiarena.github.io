Understanding the Shift
What is Open Banking & Open Finance?

Open Banking is the practice of banks exposing customer-consented account and transaction data — and payment initiation — to authorised third parties via secure APIs. Open Finance extends the same consent-driven model beyond current/savings accounts to the full spectrum of financial services: loans, investments, insurance, pensions and tax data — making the whole financial life of a customer portable, comparable and actionable.

🏦 Open Banking
Bank account data (balances, transactions) and payment initiation shared with licensed Third Party Providers (TPPs) via regulated APIs, with explicit customer consent. Powers PFM apps, lending underwriting, and account-to-account payments.
🌐 Open Finance
Widens the data perimeter to investments, insurance, pensions, credit bureaus, GST and tax records. A single consent can pull a 360° financial picture across every institution a customer deals with — not just their bank.
🔗 Embedded Finance
The next layer: financial products (credit, insurance, payments) embedded directly inside non-financial platforms — e-commerce, ERP, gig platforms — using the same open APIs, so finance shows up at the point of need.

Closed Banking

Data locked inside each bank's core system. Customers manually shared statements; switching or comparing products was slow and paper-heavy.

Open Banking

Regulator-mandated or market-led APIs (PSD2 in the EU, Account Aggregator framework in India) let consented apps read accounts and initiate payments.

Open Finance / Embedded

Data and rails span the full financial stack and are woven into everyday platforms — banking becomes a feature, not a destination.

Banks (Data Providers / ASPSPs)

Cross-Sell Intelligence

Product Velocity

Common Questions
Open Finance FAQs
What's the difference between Open Banking and Open Finance? ▼
Open Banking is scoped to bank accounts — balances, transactions and payment initiation — shared via API with consent. Open Finance is the broader umbrella: it extends the same consent-driven sharing model to investments, insurance, pensions, tax and credit bureau data, giving a full 360° financial picture rather than just a banking one.
How do banks make money if they're giving fintechs access to their data? ▼
Banks monetise in several ways: charging for premium/high-volume API access, earning distribution economics when fintech partners originate deposits or loans that settle on the bank's books, cross-selling based on richer data signals, and reducing their own cost of customer acquisition by reaching users through partner front-ends instead of building every channel themselves.
Is my data safe when it's shared across the ecosystem? ▼
Data only moves with explicit, purpose-bound consent that specifies what is shared, with whom, for how long, and for what use. Consent managers are licensed and audited, transport is encrypted end-to-end, and consent can be revoked at any time. Regulators additionally enforce data-minimisation and breach-notification requirements on every participant in the chain.
Can a fintech operate without partnering with a bank? ▼
Rarely for regulated activities like holding deposits or issuing payment instruments — those still require a licensed institution behind the scenes (Banking-as-a-Service). Open Finance lets fintechs build the customer experience and underwriting logic while a bank or NBFC partner provides the regulated balance sheet and settlement rails underneath.
What role does embedded finance play in this architecture? ▼
Embedded finance sits at the top of the stack, consuming the same consent, data and payment APIs to place financial products — credit, insurance, savings — directly inside non-financial platforms like e-commerce, payroll or ERP software, so users get financial services at the exact moment of need rather than visiting a separate banking app.
